Capstone project · 2025 to 2026

A modular steering wheel and dash for our Formula SAE EV

Our team's old dashboard ran on a Raspberry Pi. Great for trying ideas, slow to boot. For our senior capstone, four of us replaced it with a microcontroller wheel that puts the screen, the driver's controls and data logging in one unit that's ready for competition.

Drag to spin it around
01 · How it's wired

One CAN bus, through the quick release

An Adafruit Feather CAN reads the buttons and encoders through a scanned matrix and drives the TFT screen over SPI. A 12 V to 3.3 V buck-boost powers it from the car's low voltage system.

Power, CAN high and CAN low pass through pogo pins inside the quick release, so the wheel comes off without unplugging anything. On the car side it talks to the ECU and data acquisition module the team already had.

02 · What's inside

A sandwich you can take apart

  • Grips and a front plate holding the display, six buttons and two encoders
  • Aluminium spacers and a wall that close in the electronics
  • The main PCB and a back plate
  • A quick release hub at the bottom

The housings are sealed against dust and water and built to handle vibration, heat cycles, humidity and EMI.

03 · Wheel and dash

Everything the driver touches

The wheel has the screen, six push buttons and two rotary encoders.

The dash below it carries the Ready-to-Drive and Emergency Stop buttons that FSAE EV rules require, error lights, BSPD probe points and an LV battery readout. The left plate adds a voltmeter, the RTD buzzer and banana jacks for quick checks.

04 · Software

Live screens built with LVGL

Written in C and C++. It pulls ECU data off the CAN bus and shows it live: speed with accumulator and battery levels, tire pressures, and an error log. The driver flips between screens with the push buttons.

Everything is logged for testing, and each screen is its own module, so adding new data or a new screen is easy.

05 · In the car

Where it goes and what's next

It mounts on the steering column of our car, circled in red. Next up:

  • A better UI with more telemetry on screen
  • Wireless communication
  • Lower power draw and tougher housings

Formula SAE · Low voltage

Wiring the car by hand

A big part of my job on the team is building the low voltage wiring harnesses. Each one gets drawn out full size on a layout board first, then the runs are bundled with a concentric twist so they stay tough, flexible and neat inside the chassis.
